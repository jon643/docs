**Consistent chemical exfoliation with salicylic acid is the standard over-the-counter fix for closed comedones** — it dissolves the plug of oil and dead skin inside the pore so the bump can flatten and clear on its own, without squeezing. Expect 4–8 weeks of steady use before the bumps meaningfully clear.

## What closed comedones are

Closed comedones (whiteheads in the dermatological sense) are small, flesh-colored or slightly white bumps that sit under the skin's surface. They form when a pore is blocked by oil and dead skin cells but stays closed at the top — unlike a blackhead, where the open pore lets the plug oxidize and darken. They are non-inflamed, which is the good news: treated early, they clear without the redness, pain, or dark marks of an inflamed pimple.

## The routine that clears them

1. **Cleanse daily** with a salicylic acid wash such as the [Acne Face Wash 2.0](https://www.neutralyze.com/products/neutralyze-face-wash-2-0) — 2% salicylic acid (the maximum OTC strength) plus 1% mandelic acid.
2. **Add a leave-on exfoliating step.** A rinse-off wash alone works slowly; a leave-on product keeps the acids working in the pore. Swipe a [Neutralyze Exfoliating Pad](https://www.neutralyze.com/products/neutralyze-exfoliating-pads) over the affected area once daily, building to twice daily as your skin adjusts — see the [routine order guide](/guides/routine-order).
3. **Moisturize** with a non-comedogenic moisturizer so the barrier stays healthy while you exfoliate, and wear SPF 30+ in the morning.
4. **Be patient and consistent.** A brief flare in weeks 1–2 can be normal as congested pores clear — see [Purging vs. Breaking Out](/guides/purging-vs-breakout).

## What to avoid

- **Don't pick or squeeze.** Closed comedones have no opening; squeezing usually ruptures the pore wall and turns a painless bump into an inflamed pimple with a risk of dark marks.
- **Skip gritty scrubs.** Physical scrubs can't reach the plug inside the pore and can inflame the area; chemical exfoliation works deeper and more evenly.
- **Don't layer every active at once.** If you add a retinoid, alternate it with your acid routine — see [Using Salicylic Acid with Retinol](/guides/salicylic-acid-with-retinol).

## When to see a dermatologist

If the bumps haven't improved after 8+ weeks of consistent use, a dermatologist can add a prescription retinoid (adapalene, tretinoin), which normalizes how pores shed dead skin — the other first-line treatment for comedonal acne.
