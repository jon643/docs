**Mandelic acid is the better AHA choice for sensitive skin and darker skin tones; glycolic acid is stronger but more irritating.** Both exfoliate the skin's surface, but mandelic acid's much larger molecule penetrates slowly and evenly, so it delivers the benefits of an AHA — smoother texture, faded dark marks, more even tone — with a fraction of the sting.

## The key difference: molecule size

- **Glycolic acid** is the smallest AHA. It penetrates fast and deep, which makes it effective for texture and fine lines but also the most likely AHA to cause stinging, redness, and irritation.
- **Mandelic acid** is one of the largest AHAs (derived from the same family as almonds, though Neutralyze uses a synthesized, nut-allergy-safe form). It penetrates slowly, exfoliating gently and uniformly.

## Why this matters for darker skin tones

Irritation itself can trigger post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) — the dark marks that medium and deep skin tones are especially prone to after breakouts or harsh products. Because mandelic acid exfoliates with far less irritation, it carries a lower risk of rebound dark marks, which is why it is widely regarded as one of the most pigmentation-friendly exfoliating acids. Glycolic acid, used aggressively, can make PIH worse on reactive or melanin-rich skin.

## Which should you choose?

- **Acne-prone, sensitive, or darker skin:** mandelic acid, ideally paired with salicylic acid (a BHA) to clear inside the pore. See [Salicylic Acid vs. Mandelic Acid](/comparisons/salicylic-vs-mandelic) for how the two work together.
- **Resilient skin focused purely on texture or fine lines, with no active breakouts:** glycolic acid can work — start low and slow.
