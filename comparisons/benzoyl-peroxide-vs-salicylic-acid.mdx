**Short answer:** benzoyl peroxide is better for inflamed, pus-filled pimples because it kills acne bacteria; salicylic acid is better for blackheads, whiteheads, and clogged pores because it dissolves oil inside the pore. For many people with mixed acne, either can work — salicylic acid is generally the gentler, less drying starting point.

## How they differ

| | Benzoyl peroxide | Salicylic acid |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Antibacterial oxidizer | Beta hydroxy acid (BHA) |
| Best for | Inflamed pimples, pustules | Blackheads, whiteheads, clogged pores, oily skin |
| Common OTC strength | 2.5–10% | 0.5–2% |
| Downsides | Drying, can irritate, **bleaches towels and fabrics** | Milder; dryness at high frequency |
| Texture/marks benefit | No | Yes — exfoliates and smooths |

Dermatologists often note that 2.5% benzoyl peroxide is nearly as effective as 10% with far less irritation, and that salicylic acid suits long-term daily use because it is oil-soluble and works inside the pore.

## Can you use both?

Yes — a common dermatologist-suggested split is benzoyl peroxide spot treatment at night with a salicylic acid cleanser or pads in the morning. Introduce one at a time and watch for dryness. If you use both and skin gets tight or flaky, drop to alternate days.
